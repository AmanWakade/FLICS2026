TitleLeveraging Federated Learning for Predicting Kidney Graft Survival Using OPTN Dataset
Paper(Mar 10, 16:01)
TrackMain Track 1 – Federated Learning Systems & Applications
Author keywords"Kidney Transplantation".
"Graft Survival Prediction".
"Federated Learning".
EasyChair keyphraseskidney transplantation (100), kidney graft survival prediction (100), graft survival prediction (95), long term graft survival (80), data local training (79), global model (70), model parameters (70), balanced accuracy (70), transplant centers (70), findings demonstrated (70), clinical decision making (63), machine learning models (63), kidney graft failure (63), federated optimization algorithms (63), predictive performance (60), roc auc (60), derby derby united kingdom (60), college of science and engineering university (51), science and engineering university of derby (51), renal dysfunction and long term graft (51), dysfunction and long term graft failure (51), learning system for kidney graft survival (51), system for kidney graft survival prediction (51), deep learning (50), central server (50), centralized model (50), graft loss (50), registry data (50), post transplant (50), transplant outcomes (50), transplant dataset (50)
AbstractPredicting kidney graft survival is crucial for
enhancing post-transplant results and assisting with clinical
decision-making. Improved post-transplant care and better clinical decision-making can be supported by early one-year graft
survival prediction. However, the majority of predictive models
currently in use rely on centralized datasets, necessitating the
sharing of private patient information between institutions. This
study uses the Organ Procurement Transplantation Network
(OPTN) transplant dataset to propose a Federated Learning
(FL) system for one-year kidney graft failure prediction. The
system mimics a multi-hospital setting in which five transplant
centers work together to train a Multi-Layer Perceptron model
while maintaining patient data locally at each facility. FedAvg,
FedProx, and Stochastic Controlled Averaging (SCAFFOLD)
are three federated optimisation algorithms that are assessed
and contrasted with a centralised baseline model. According
to experimental results, federated methods like FedAvg and
FedProx preserve data privacy while achieving performance
comparable to centralised training. These results show how
federated learning can be used for predictive modelling in kidney
transplant outcome analysis while maintaining privacy
